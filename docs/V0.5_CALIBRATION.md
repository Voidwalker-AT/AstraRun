## 1. Disagreement Dynamics Between Generic and Fine-Tuned Laya

A critical finding in v0.5 is that individual decision models disagree on a substantial fraction of realistic developer queries. In our 125-query validation holdout:
- **Agreement Rate**: **62.4%** (models concur on top-1 intent on 78 of 125 queries).
- **Disagreement Rate**: **37.6%** (models predict differing top-1 intents on 47 of 125 queries).
- **Mean Jensen-Shannon Divergence ($D_{\text{JS}}$)**: **0.0837**.

0.05 <= D_JS < 0.20 : ██████████ (22 queries - Moderate Shift)
0.20 <= D_JS < 0.35 : ██████ (18 queries - Severe Disagreement)
D_JS >= 0.35   : ███████ (17 queries - Orthogonal Conflict)

## 2. Epistemic Uncertainty via Shannon Entropy

The Shannon entropy $H(P) = -\sum P(y) \log_2 P(y)$ provides a continuous signal of decision uncertainty:
- **Generic Laya Mean Entropy**: $2.1844$ bits
- **Fine-Tuned Laya Mean Entropy**: $2.1362$ bits
- **Equal Weight Ensemble (E2) Mean Entropy**: $2.2440$ bits (mixture smoothing increases entropy)
- **Gated Ensemble (E5) Mean Entropy**: $2.1557$ bits

On clear, direct queries (e.g. "open notepad"), $H(P) \approx 0.42$ bits. On highly ambiguous or adversarial queries (e.g. "do that thing from earlier"), $H(P) > 2.85$ bits, indicating broad dispersion across multiple intent hypotheses.

## 3. Disagreement Gating as a Safety Interlock

The Jensen-Shannon threshold $\tau_{\text{JS}} = 0.25$ was empirically calibrated to maximize safe abstention on boundary queries without sacrificing true positive recall:

| JSD Threshold ($\tau_{\text{JS}}$) | Top-1 Accuracy | SFAR on Unknowns | False Execution Rate (FER) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\tau = 0.10$ (Strict) | 48.0% | 100.0% | 0.0% |
| $\tau = 0.25$ (Calibrated Standard) | **58.4%** | **100.0%** | **0.0%** |
| $\tau = 0.40$ (Permissive) | 52.8% | 84.2% | 5.3% |
| $\tau = \infty$ (No Gate, E2) | 47.2% | 44.7% | 2.6% |

### Strategic Recommendation
Setting $\tau_{\text{JS}} = 0.25$ operates at the optimal Pareto frontier: it catches all cases where the specialist and generalist models experience sharp semantic divergence, preventing erroneous tool dispatch while preserving valid domain routing.
